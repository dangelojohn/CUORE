Why this bolt fails — step by step
  1. Winter road salt spray enters driver wheel well and migrates up into engine bay. Salt-laden meltwater channels toward lowest point: the chassis C-rail below the ABS module.
  2. The scuttle drain rubber nipple, when partially blocked by leaf debris, backs up and overflows. Water exits from the scuttle tray and drips directly onto the taped loom running above the G102 stud.
  3. The loom tape (PVC factory wrap) is not waterproof — it is merely an abrasion wrap. Capillary action pulls salt water into the loom, which wicks along all four ground wire strands to their ring terminals.
  4. Zinc chromate plating on the ring terminals dissolves in salt water over one to two winters. Green copper oxide and white zinc salt crystals form between the terminal face and the chassis metal contact surface.
  5. Contact resistance rises from <0.01Ω to 0.5–2Ω. This adds a voltage offset to every module that references this stud as its chassis return, triggering the observed DTC cascade.

Why exactly the driver mic and speakers die — and not anything else

The DCM (door control module) LIN bus is a low-voltage (12V) serial bus. The DCM's chassis reference for LIN communication is the BK/YL wire terminating at G103. When G103 resistance rises, the LIN bus reference floats — the DCM remains powered but its LIN communication becomes unreliable. The audio amplifier in the door sees its negative rail rise by the voltage drop amount. Speaker output collapses because the return path is resistive. The microphone is a condenser type — its shield ground is the BK/RD wire at G103. A floating mic shield picks up RF interference and common-mode noise, presenting as silence or static. All of this is specific to G103 because the passenger side G105 is on a separate, unaffected circuit.

Step-by-Step Elimination Procedure — Strict Order
Step 1 — Visual G102
NO TOOLS
What to do

Open hood. Driver side, behind headlight. Reach behind and below ABS hydraulic unit toward chassis rail. Shine flashlight on M8 bolt head with ring terminal stack.

GOOD: Bright metal bolt head, clean ring terminals, dry loom tape
FAULTY: Green/white powder on terminals, orange staining on chassis, swollen tape
If faulty: proceed to Step 3 (cleaning). If visually OK: proceed to Step 2 (voltage test).
Step 2 — Voltage Drop G102
DVOM REQUIRED
How to test

Engine running. Headlights on, blower max, brake pedal pressed. DVOM DC millivolts. Negative probe: battery negative post. Positive probe: G102 chassis bolt head.

GOOD: < 50mV. G102 clean, move to G103.
FAULTY: > 100mV. High resistance confirmed. G102 is root cause.
50–100mV = marginal. Clean and retest. Any reading confirms or eliminates G102.
Step 3 — Clean G102
13MM SOCKET
How to clean
  1. Disconnect battery negative before touching any ground stud.
  2. Remove M8 bolt. Count and photograph the terminal stack order before removing.
  3. Clean chassis contact face with 120-grit sandpaper to bare metal. Circular motion, 30 seconds.
  4. Clean each ring terminal face with wire brush or emery cloth. Remove all green oxide.
  5. Apply thin coat of dielectric grease to contact faces.
  6. Reinstall terminals in original order. Torque M8 bolt to 8 N·m (6 ft-lbs).
  7. Wrap loom above stud with self-amalgamating silicone tape (NOT PVC). Overlap 50%.
After cleaning: proceed to Step 4 (G103 visual).
Step 4 — Visual + Drop G103
KICK PANEL OFF
Access

Pull driver kick panel (2 push-pins, pull straight toward you). M8 stud at A-pillar base is immediately visible — 3 ring terminals, BK/YL, BK, BK/RD.

GOOD: < 50mV drop. Driver door audio fault is elsewhere (connector).
FAULTY: > 80mV. G103 confirmed — clean same as Step 3 procedure.
This single reading explains or eliminates driver audio + mic failure.
Step 5 — Verify G1 Terminal Faces
RESISTANCE CHECK
Test

DVOM in resistance (Ω). Battery disconnected. Probe battery negative terminal to transmission-side M10 bolt head. Repeat for chassis-side bolt head.

GOOD: < 0.1Ω each end.
MARGINAL: 0.1–0.3Ω. Clean contact face to bare metal and retest.
U1960 stored is legacy from old strap event — will not return after battery reset if terminal faces are clean.
Step 6 — Battery Reset + Rescan
FINAL STEP
Procedure
  1. Disconnect battery negative. Wait 20 minutes minimum (allows all module RAM to clear).
  2. Reconnect. Start engine. Turn steering wheel lock-to-lock × 2 slowly.
  3. Let idle 5 minutes with all lights on.
  4. Rescan with Launch or AlfaOBD. All U-codes and B-codes from ground event should be absent.
  5. B10AA-4A (PROXI) will NOT self-clear. Requires AlfaOBD software → Body → PROXI Alignment. One-time fix.
Any code that returns after the reset = remaining hardware issue at a specific module. Do not replace modules until after this reset confirms what survives.
FIRE RISK WARNING — ACT NOW

The documented failure mode for a fully corroded G102 is not just module errors — it is thermal damage. When the G102 stud loses contact entirely, 200A starter return current is rerouted through the thin-gauge ABS sensor ground wires (BK/GN, 0.5mm² wire). These wires are not rated for starter current. They glow red-hot within seconds of a start attempt. The headlight harness from bumper to dash has been documented as melting in this failure mode, with repair quotes of $3,500–$10,000+. One owner had State Farm cover it under "electrical fire." You are currently in the early-resistance phase. The window to fix this cheaply is now — before the next cold start.

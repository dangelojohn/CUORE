DESIGN CRITIQUE — PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE FINDINGS
This analysis confirms that the Giorgio platform's ground network architecture contains a systemic maintenance gap that is deterministically exploited by salt-market operating conditions. Specific findings per SAE ARP4761 design review criteria:

Finding F-01: Chassis ground studs G102 and G103 are tin-plated copper ring terminals torqued to bare sheet metal with no corrosion protection coating, no sealed enclosure, and no maintenance instruction in the scheduled service documentation. This is an FMECA oversight — high criticality failure modes (Class I: fire) are not addressed by any preventive maintenance action in the published service schedule.

Finding F-02: The scuttle drain exit point is co-located with the highest-current, highest-criticality ground cluster (G102) without any physical separation or shielding. This violates the "single point of failure" principle for safety-critical circuits — a single blocked drain can initiate the fire event chain.

Finding F-03: The Bosch S5 AGM battery replacement was performed correctly and is confirmed functional. Battery replacement is in the service schedule. Ground network inspection is not. The asymmetry in scheduled maintenance coverage means a customer who follows the Alfa Romeo service schedule exactly can still develop a Class I fire risk.

Finding F-04: Recall U36 (NHTSA 18V205000) addressed BCM connector sealing — the passenger-side water path — but did not address G102 or G103. This is confirmed by the fact that U36-remedied vehicles continue to develop G102 failures at the same rate as unremedied vehicles in salt markets.
